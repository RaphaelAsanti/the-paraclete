The Paraclete

God is the Judge. Everyone is on trial. The Paraclete was the lawyer and comforter requested by Jesus.

The Paraclete’s Judicial Role and Comfort

In the divine judicial order, God is the Judge, satan is the accuser, and the Paraclete is the judicially appointed Adviser who provides the accused with precise instruction from the Judge. Because God is Merciful, the Paraclete’s appointment also includes the role of comforter, offering stabilizing support and certain benefits while the accused works through the requirements of their case. He is not a defense lawyer, but a commissioned Adviser whose guidance and presence ensure fairness, clarity, and mercy throughout the process.

The Paraclete in the Divine Judicial Order

In the divine judicial structure, God is the Judge, humanity is the accused, satan is the accuser, and the Paraclete is the judicially appointed Adviser. Satan, as accuser, is a fallen angel not bound by ethical constraints, whose desire is to lead as many as possible into hell with him. To ensure perfect fairness and justice, God appoints the Paraclete to instruct the accused precisely in what the Judge requires. The Paraclete is not a defense lawyer, but a commissioned Adviser who speaks only what God gives, so that no one can claim ignorance when judgment is rendered.

The Paraclete as Comforter

Because God is Merciful, the Paraclete’s judicial appointment includes a role of comfort. While the accused works through the details of their case, the Paraclete provides stabilizing benefits, guidance, and reassurance. He is not a defense lawyer, but a divinely commissioned Adviser whose presence offers support during the process of compliance with the Judge’s requirements. This comfort is part of God’s fairness and justice, ensuring the accused is never left alone while fulfilling the instructions given by the Paraclete.

Jesus’s Promises About the Comforter

Jesus promised that the Comforter would come after His departure. He stated that the Father would send the Comforter in His name, and that, if the people followed the advice carefully - the Comforter would remain with the people forever. This was not presented as an optional or symbolic event; it was a formal declaration that a new Adviser would be appointed to guide the people after Jesus completed His own mission and its required terms.

Jesus also explained the rules associated with the Comforter’s arrival. He said that the Comforter would teach, remind, and guide, but that the people were required to listen carefully and obey. The Comforter carried the same authority that the Angel of the Presence carried in the desert, and Jesus made it clear that rebellion against the Comforter would not be forgiven. These rules established the legal framework for how the Comforter’s guidance was to be received.

Jesus further stated that the Comforter could not arrive until He Himself departed. His departure was the trigger condition for the Comforter’s appointment. Once Jesus left, the Comforter would be sent to continue the work of advising the people, ensuring that they had access to the guidance required to resolve their legal difficulties with the Judge.

These promises and rules formed the foundation for the Paraclete’s mission. The people were expected to follow the Comforter’s instructions, accept His guidance, and allow Him to straighten out their legal problems. Rejecting the Comforter meant rejecting the very help Jesus had arranged for them, and it placed them directly in conflict with the requirements set by the Judge.

The Two Missions: Angel of the Presence and the Paraclete

The mission of the Angel of the Presence was to lead the Israelites through the desert and provide God’s guidance so they could be straightened out with God and learn His Ways on the journey to the promised land. This forty-year period served as a dry run of the later mission, four thousand years after, when the Paraclete was appointed. The Paraclete continues the same category of work: a judicially appointed Adviser who provides God’s instruction and comfort while the accused work through the requirements set by the Judge.

The comfort given to the Israelites in the desert was practical and ongoing: their clothing never wore out, their tents and supplies remained intact, and their food and water were continually provided. This sustained support was part of the Angel’s appointed role, ensuring the people could complete their long journey without collapse. In the same way, the Paraclete provides stabilizing benefits for those under his guidance, reflecting God’s mercy during the process of being straightened out with Him.

The warnings attached to both missions were essentially the same. God told Moses not to rebel against the Angel of the Presence because he carried the Judge’s authority and would not forgive transgression. Jesus later delivered the same category of warning about the Holy Spirit, but expressed it in a more gentle and kindly manner. The underlying instruction remained identical: rejecting the appointed Adviser is rejecting the Judge Himself.

Enoch’s Mission as an Early Paraclete

Enoch from the Old Testament functioned as an early Paraclete. Although little is recorded about his life beyond the statement that he “walked with God,” he left behind the Book of the Watchers, which documented the sins and corruption of the elite in his era. Because the law and formal rules had not yet been established, Enoch’s role did not involve issuing warnings. His mission was primarily to record the wrongdoing of the time as part of the early process of bringing the generation into alignment with God.

God’s pattern is to visit the sins of the fathers on the third and fourth generations, and Noah appeared three and a half generations after Enoch as the instrument of judgment. This establishes the early structure of how God sends an appointed messenger to provide documentation or guidance, followed by a later act of correction. Enoch’s mission was an early prototype of the later missions of the Angel of the Presence and, four thousand years after, the Paraclete.

Satan as the Accuser

Satan’s role has always been that of the accuser, but in this era he has taken a different approach. Instead of openly accusing the accused, he befriends them and assures them they are not in trouble at all. This is simply a variation of the same strategy he has used throughout history: misleading the accused so they do not recognize their condition or their need to be straightened out with God.

His deception goes further by telling the accused that they were meant to rule the world, repeating the same promise he used in the beginning. He flatters them, tells them they are exceptional, and claims he will help them achieve greatness. Anyone who attempts to advise them is portrayed as the “mean old God” trying to enslave them. This inversion of accusation into false friendship is designed to keep the accused unaware of their actual standing before the Judge.

Beneath all of this is satan’s personal mission: to take everyone to hell with him. His strategy of befriending, flattering, and reassuring the accused serves this single goal. By convincing them they are safe, powerful, and in no need of guidance, he prevents them from listening to the Adviser appointed by the Judge. This ensures they remain on the path that leads to the same destination he is bound for.

The promise that they were meant to rule the world is the same temptation satan used on Jesus during His forty days in the desert before beginning His own mission. Jesus resisted and succeeded. People today, however, sell their soul for trivial things in a matter of minutes, falling for the same lie that Jesus overcame. People did not learn from Jesus’s lesson, and satan’s strategy remains effective because the accused accept the flattery without recognizing the trap.

The Paraclete’s Arrival and Opposition

The Paraclete arrived with the mandate to establish himself in the land and then advise the people so they could be straightened out from the legal difficulties they had with God. His mission was to provide the Judge’s guidance, just as the Angel of the Presence had done in the desert, and to help the accused work through the requirements set before them.

Satan made certain this could not happen. Instead of allowing the Paraclete to carry out his mission, satan blocked him at every turn. He also recruited some of God’s other servants to his own team, using their reputation and renown as a shield to hide behind. By doing this, satan ensured that the Paraclete’s authority would be obscured, his presence minimized, and his mission delayed or disrupted.

Villifying the Paraclete and knocking him down made it easier for the people to believe satan’s lies. Once the Paraclete’s credibility was damaged, the accused were far more willing to accept satan’s flattery, promises, and reassurances. With the Adviser’s voice weakened, satan’s narrative became the dominant one, allowing him to keep the people unaware of their actual standing before the Judge.

The People’s Response to Satan’s Strategy

The people loved satan’s strategy. According to satan and his angels, they were awesome, destined for greatness, and meant to rule the world. They were promised success in trade, industry, politics, and entertainment, along with wealth, influence, and recognition. These promises made satan’s narrative extremely appealing, and the people embraced the idea that they were chosen for power and prominence.

It did not take long for them to escalate the tearing down of the Paraclete. Satan had accused the Paraclete of being the accuser rather than the adviser, and once the people accepted satan’s flattery, they were eager to believe anything that diminished the Paraclete’s authority. By attacking and discrediting the Paraclete, the people reinforced satan’s lies and ensured that the Adviser’s voice would be ignored.

As a result, the legal problems the people had with the Judge increased rather than lessened as time went on. By rejecting the Adviser and embracing satan’s promises, they moved further away from the requirements set before them. Their condition worsened because the very guidance meant to straighten them out was the guidance they chose to silence. The more they tore down the Paraclete, the deeper their legal difficulties became.
